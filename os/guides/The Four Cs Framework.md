The Four Cs Framework

The Three Ms are how you think. The Four Cs are how you build. This guide covers the framework I use to set up every AI OS: Context, Connections, Capabilities, and Cadence. They stack in order and you can't skip ahead.

The second half is the most important pre-build exercise in the entire system. Before you touch Claude Code, you map your tools across the seven tier-one buckets every business has. This is the map that tells you which connections to wire up first.

The Four Cs

The four pillars of an AI OS, in order:

Context. What the AI knows about you. Your team, your tools, your voice, your business, your money, your priorities.

Connections. What data it can reach. Claude Code on its own can search the web, but it can't see your Monday.com or your Drive. You have to wire those in.

Capabilities. What it can produce. Not just a knowledge base. It needs to be able to do useful work.

Cadence. When it acts on its own. Scheduled tasks. Loops. Things that run while you sleep.

These build on each other. You can't have cadence without connections. You can't have capabilities without context. Go 1, 2, 3, 4 in order.

How to test each pillar

Open a fresh Claude Code session and ask a question. Does it answer like a teammate, or like a stranger who just met you five seconds ago? That's your context test.

Ask it to pull a list of overdue Monday.com tasks. Does it have access? That's your connections test.

Ask it to write a LinkedIn post from a YouTube video. Does the output sound like you? That's your capabilities test.

Close your laptop, go to sleep, and wake up to a daily roundup waiting for you. That's your cadence test.

We have a built-in /audit skill that scores you on all four. We'll get to that.

The seven tier-one buckets

Before you wire anything up, you sketch out the seven things every business has to track. Grab a piece of paper or open a doc. Don't go straight into Claude Code with this.

Revenue. Stripe, Circle, QuickBooks, whatever payment processor or membership platform you use.

Customer. Where your customers actually live. CRM, community platform, SaaS dashboard.

Calendar. For me this is just Google Calendar. Everything syncs there.

Comms. Where you communicate. Gmail, Slack, Monday.com updates.

Tasks. Your project management. Monday.com, Notion, Linear, whatever.

Meetings. Where your meeting transcripts live. Fireflies, Otter, Granola.

Knowledge. Everything else important. Video transcripts, Drive docs, local files, brain dumps.

For each bucket, write down which tools you use. That list is your connection roadmap.

The "ask my AI" test

Here's how I know if I've mapped enough tools. If someone wanted to get a hold of me and ask a question about my business, my AI executive assistant could probably answer it faster and more accurately than I could. Because it has the same data I do, plus perfect memory, plus it never sleeps.

If your AI could pass that test for the majority of questions your team or your customers might ask, you've mapped enough to start building. If not, write down what's missing.

Key takeaways

Four Cs in order: Context, Connections, Capabilities, Cadence.

Each one builds on the previous one. No skipping.

Map your tools across the seven tier-one buckets before you build.

The bar is: my AI could answer questions about my business better than I could.
