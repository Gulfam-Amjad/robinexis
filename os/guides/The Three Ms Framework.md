The Three Ms Framework

You can have the best tools in the world and still fail at this if your mindset isn't right. This guide covers the Three Ms framework that runs underneath everything we'll build later: Mindset, Method, and Machine.

We'll go deep on three habits that have to become reflexive. The default shift. The function breakdown. The curiosity rule. Then we'll talk about the part nobody warns you about, which is that productivity drops about 20% before it climbs. Most people quit during the dip. I want you to know it's coming so you don't.

Why mindset goes first

Before you ever touch Claude Code, you have to upgrade the way you think about AI. The Three Ms framework is how I think about all of this. Mindset is the lens. Method is how you decide what to automate. Machine is the actual build.

Everything in the rest of this guide assumes you've internalized these three habits. If you skip this, the build won't stick.

Habit 1: The default shift

Before you do any task, just ask yourself: how could AI do this? Or at least, how could AI do 30% of it? The truth is AI isn't going to do everything 100%. But 75% is still a massive win. Even 30% is a real productivity gain.

Here's a real example. The other day I needed to update over 300 YouTube description links. Old me would have just said "this sucks but I have to do it" and clicked through each video manually. Probably an hour of work.

New me, the default is no, I'm not doing that. Let me brainstorm with Claude Code and figure out how to make it happen. Maybe it's an API, maybe an MCP, maybe even just a browser automation script. The point is the default is now stubborn. If anything sounds boring or repetitive, I'm not doing it. The AI OS is.

Habit 2: The function breakdown

Your role is a set of functions. Everything you do can be broken into a tiny task and you can automate one piece at a time.

Take making a YouTube video. That's a tall task to automate end-to-end. But break it into pieces. Ideation. Scripting. Slide creation. Packaging titles and thumbnails. Descriptions. Comment replies. Now each of those is a chunk you can tackle on its own.

I can definitely automate ideation, or at least 95% of it. I can automate slide creation. I can automate descriptions. When you have the mindset of breaking the work into baby steps, it becomes way more achievable.

And here's the bonus. Each of those chunks is reusable. The slide deck chunk for a YouTube video can get slotted into your meeting prep flow. The chunks compose. That mindset matters.

Habit 3: The curiosity rule

Never accept AI output without asking why. Treat AI as a mentor, not a vending machine.

There's this idea of "dark code" floating around right now. So many people are using AI to write code that they don't actually understand what each block of code is doing. You don't have to be able to read Python line by line. But you do need to understand why something was built a certain way and what would happen if X happened.

The other piece of curiosity is just asking. If you don't know whether something is possible to automate, ask. Don't assume the answer is no.

Productivity drops before it climbs

Here's the part nobody warns you about. When you make a change like this, you should expect about a 20% productivity dip for the first few days. That's just how change works.

The question is whether the change is worth it. Will it eventually pay back more than that 20%? Yes. The upside on an AI OS is closer to a 50%+ gain once you're past the curve.

Same goes for the learning curve. People think learning is linear. It isn't. Learning is exponential. When you start something new, you're below the line you would have been on if you just kept doing it the old way. But by day three or four, you cross that line. By day five you're way ahead.

The gap between day one and day three is where people give up. Don't.

Key takeaways

Mindset isn't motivation. It's the lens that finds the leverage in every task.

The question is never "will AI do this?" It's "to what extent can AI do this?"

Your job is a tree of tasks. Vague goals get realistic when you chunk them.

Treat AI as a mentor. Push back on its answers. Ask why.

Push through the 20% dip. The 50% gain is on the other side.
